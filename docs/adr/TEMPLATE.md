# ADR-NNNN: Decision Title

- **Status:** Proposed | Accepted | Superseded | Deprecated
- **Recorded:** YYYY-MM-DD
- **Decision provenance:** Contemporary | Retrospective reconstruction
- **Related requirements:** Relevant requirements and links
- **Implementation evidence:** Relevant files and results, if any

> **Using this template.** Copy this file to `NNNN-short-title.md`, using the next free four-digit number and a lowercase, hyphenated title, and replace every guidance block. Aim for about one page; normally no more than two. Link to detailed design documents instead of copying them. See the [ADR index](README.md) for the lifecycle and conventions.
>
> **Header fields.**
> - **Status:** start new decisions as *Proposed*. When an ADR is replaced, write "Superseded by ADR-NNNN" and link to the replacing file; the replacing ADR adds a **Supersedes** line.
> - **Recorded:** the date this ADR was written, not the date of an earlier informal decision.
> - **Decision provenance:** *Contemporary* if the ADR was written when the decision was made. *Retrospective reconstruction* if the decision was made earlier: name the original note and when it was first committed, and state that the options were reconstructed for comparison rather than formally evaluated at the time.
> - **Related requirements:** requirement IDs (for example FR-03) and rules from [requirements](../requirements.md).
> - **Implementation evidence:** source files, migrations, and recorded test or experiment results, or "None". Evidence shows what was observed under stated conditions; it is not a guarantee.
> - **Optional lines:** **Legacy reference** for an earlier informal decision note; **Supersedes** for a replaced ADR.

## Problem

> The issue or pain point, in two to four sentences. What goes wrong, or what cannot be done, without a decision? Describe the problem, not the solution.

## Constraints

> What limits the options: requirements, existing decisions, data, security and tenant boundaries, environment, skills, cost, or time. Include only constraints that actually shaped the choice.

## Options Considered

> Two or three realistic, technically credible approaches, including the one chosen. For each, give its main benefits and costs in this project's context. Do not invent benchmarks, meetings, or approvals; if an option was not evaluated experimentally, do not imply that it was.

## Decision

> The chosen option, stated so that a reviewer can check whether the code follows it, and why it was chosen over the alternatives. If parts of the decision are accepted but not yet implemented, say so explicitly.

## Risks and Consequences

> Tradeoffs consciously accepted, what could go wrong, and the follow-up work or constraints the decision creates. Include negative consequences, not only benefits.

## Revisit When

> Concrete conditions that should trigger a review: new requirements, new writers or interfaces, measured results, or changed context. Do not invent numeric thresholds that have not been measured; name the measurement that is needed instead.
